🎓 College Viva / Project Architecture Notes

Why MongoDB NoSQL for Food Rescue?

  • Flexible Schema: Surplus food items have varying attributes (expiry dates, pickup time slots, allergens, dietary flags, packaging types) which naturally fit document structures without rigid relational table migrations.
  • Atomic Operations: The food claiming feature leverages MongoDB's find_one_and_update to atomically ensure no two food seekers can claim the same surplus meal concurrently.
  • High Write & Query Speed: Ideal for real-time location-based searching, filtering by food status, and tracking fast-moving perishable food items.

MongoDB Collections Schema:

  • users: _id, username, email, password (PBKDF2 hashed), full_name, phone, default_role (provider/seeker), organization, address, created_at, updated_at
  • food_items: _id, provider_id, provider_name, provider_phone, title, description, category, food_type, quantity, pickup_address, pickup_city, pickup_time_slot, expiry_info, status (available/claimed/collected/cancelled), claimed_by_id, claimed_at, collected_at, created_at, updated_at
  • claims: _id, food_id, food_title, provider_id, seeker_id, seeker_name, seeker_phone, status, notes, claimed_at, collected_at
